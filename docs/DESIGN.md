# Prairie Engine Works: the game as built

Written from the code at version 1.76.28.1 (October 2026), and kept up to date since; the counts below were re-checked at 1.76.59.1 (November 2026). Every number here was counted in the source. Where a count comes from one table, the table is named so it can be re-checked.

## What it is

A physics-based engine builder with a career mode, by Longlight Interactive. The player designs an engine part by part, runs it on a dyno, finds out what breaks, then takes it racing or sells it. It is one web page that runs on phones and desktops and installs as an app that works offline.

## The loop

1. **Build.** Pick a layout and set 73 settings (`DEFAULT_CFG`): bore and stroke, materials, pistons, rods, crank, valvetrain, induction, boost, exhaust, fuel, launch control, oiling, cooling, sealing and finish. A 3D engine on a stand follows every choice.
2. **Test.** Run a dyno pull, an endurance run to failure or an over-rev test (`DYNO_T`). Read power and torque curves, the verdict, the failure that stopped it, and the engineer's notes.
3. **Race.** Take the engine to an event. Each one has its own time-stepped sim, classes, rivals, a 3D scene and a live HUD.
4. **Career.** Supply one race team's engines a season at a time, race your own engines at the events for purses and rep, pay crew, buy upgrades, chase reputation. Or skip the money in the Test cell (sandbox) and build anything.

The results rail (`app/rail.js`) keeps build, dyno and verdict in sight so the build-dyno-build loop never leaves the tab: a third column on desktop, a peek sheet over the tab bar on a phone.

## Starting out

The intro screen (`html/body.html`) asks for a layout, Phone or Desktop, then a mode:

- **Test cell**: everything unlocked, no money, for testing theories.
- **Career**: $20,000, a chassis dyno and five stock starter engines. Build engines for race teams a season at a time, race the events, grow the shop.

Until 1.76.32 there was a third mode, Pit Crew; since 1.76.33.0 its team seasons are the heart of the career (below), and a Pit Crew save opens as a fresh career with its titles in the log. Careers from before 1.76.33.0 (contracts, the buyer market, warranty, build bays, Dyno day) also start fresh.

An onboarding tour and "First Fire" walk a new player through the first engine (`app/help.js`). Since 1.76.114.0 the career tour is four steps, each a thing to do (sign a team, pick a starter engine, certify it, go racing), worded with the names on the buttons; the last step names the button that moves the season on and says in one line where Events, Analysis, Sponsors and the Shop are. The help file also holds a tap-to-explain glossary and 17 guided lessons (`LESSONS`). Since 1.76.115.0 a boat that breaks names the part that let go; a ready Race round button is the bright one; a team round's card says "Rep for the round" and shows any goal bonus the round earned beside it (the goal toast names the rep too); and during First Fire the header reads First fire / practice engine instead of Test cell.

## Screens

Six tabs (`#tab-*` in `html/body.html`): **Build**, **Test**, **Events**, **Analysis**, **Team**, **Shop**.

- **Build**: 14 accordion groups (`FORM`): Engine layout, Bore & stroke, Rotors & housings, Block & head materials, Pistons & rings, Rods & crank, Valvetrain, Induction & boost, Exhaust system, Fuel & tune, Launch control, Lubrication & cooling, Sealing, Finish. Base-engine list (presets), blueprints, and Build > Swap for putting the engine in a car.
- **Test**: the dyno. Environment panel (ambient, altitude), the three tests, charts, the dyno tune map editor.
- **Events**: opens on a full-screen programme (`app/evhub.js`, `css/ui-evhub.css`): one big card per event with its banner, venue, classes, purse (career) or free entry (sandbox) and your best or status, and a lock with the reason when career hasn't opened it. A card goes into that event's own page: a header bar with a Back button, then Setup, Advanced and Results panes (the old chip row is gone from view). Back returns to the grid, and the phone's Back button does the same; it is held while a run is going. The entered event is kept across tab switches in a session but not saved, so a fresh load starts on the grid. The card banners are CSS for now; `EV_BANNER` takes one image per event. Running an event turns the page into the run view (`app/runview.js`): the 3D scene fills the screen with a live HUD.
- **Analysis**: combo interactions (part pairings that help or hurt), telemetry, achievements, records, the teardown panel after a failure (it names the one change that clears the failed part without breaking anything else, trying every part table and a few slider steps, with a "Use it" button that fits it).
- **Team** (career; `#tab-market` in the markup): a strip with the engine's state for the team, the next round and Close week (after a round it reads To race week and closes the off week too, with 1 wk beside it to close just one), then three sections: Team (the season numbers, offers, the season card (which carries its own Close week / To race week button until the round is due, then Race round), standings, the last race day), Sponsors & Rivals, and Log (ledger and goals).
- **Shop**: in career, upgrades, staff, R&D, prestige items, the bank, Saves. In the Test cell it is a bench (current build next to every blueprint) and a parts catalogue (`app/shopsb.js`).

Also: a Settings pane (sound, display, graphics tier, theme, haptics, save slots, install), **photo mode** (free camera, light and backdrop presets, a still frame out, with a build sheet card), and the **trophy room** (a 3D mezzanine of what the shop has won).

## Systems

### The engine sim (`src/sim/`, `ENGINE_SIM`)

Plain JavaScript with no DOM and no three.js; it runs in node. `simulate(cfg, env)` goes atmosphere, geometry, volumetric efficiency, boost model, calibration (fuel and ignition), then the cycle (four-stroke, the rotary cycle or the two-stroke cycle), the exhaust system, component loads, the tests, reliability, life and over-rev, and mass, cost and economy. It returns power and torque curves plus everything the UI reads (verdict, failure, loads, notes). The export object has 167 members.

Content in the sim:

| Table | Count | What |
|---|---|---|
| `LAYOUTS` | 19 | single, parallel twin, inline 3 to 6, V4, V6, V8 crossplane, V8 flat-plane, V10, V12, flat 4, flat 6, 1- to 4-rotor Wankels, the Deltic triangle (two-stroke only) |
| `OPT` | 56 tables, 268 options | every part choice: block and head materials, pistons, rings, rods, cranks, bearings, valves, springs, intakes, 7 aspiration types, 7 turbo sizes, 15 fuels, injectors, pumps, diesel hardware, rotary ports and seals, two-stroke scavenging and pipes, oil, cooling, exhaust, mufflers, launch control, 6 finishes |
| `PRESETS` | 96 | base engines modelled on real ones, with their fuel |
| `combos()` | n/a | the material and part interactions shown in Analysis, with two-stroke and rotary variants |
| tune map | n/a | `mapAxis`, `mapGrid`, `mapAutoTune` and friends: a dyno tune map by rpm and load |

Fuels cover pump grades, race grades, oxygenated fuels, E85 and flex blends, methanol, propane and diesel (`FUEL_RANK`, `fitFuel`).

### Events (`src/sim/<event>.js` with `src/app/<event>.js` and `src/vis/<event>.js`)

`EV_T` lists ten: `drag`, `bracket`, `pull_sled`, `salt`, `bog`, `boat`, `road`, `hill`, `oval`, `race24`.

Weather (`app/weather.js`, since 1.76.83.0): the road test, the hill climb and the pull each get a day, a new one each visit in the test cell and one a week in a career. The day sets the air temperature and humidity the engine breathes. On the road and the hill it can rain (wet 0–1 takes grip off the tyres through `SIM.roadWetMu`, slicks most; the hill's rivals slow in it too), and on the pull it makes the dirt damp or dry. The test cell has New day and a Standard day switch; a career always has weather.

Home venues (the names on the event cards, since 1.76.106.0; the salt flats and the big hill were Bonneville and Pikes Peak until 1.76.107.0, now the made-up Bitterwater Salt Flats and Cloudsplitter Peak, with the same course and air): Section Line Dragway (the strip), Elevator Row Fairgrounds (the pull), Gumbo Flats (the bog), Cutbank Reach (the boats), Grid Road Speedway (the clay 3/8) and Sundog Speedway (the paved half-mile), with the road circuits Prairie Sky Raceway, Coulee Park and Grain Exchange. Team seasons also travel to other made-up sites (the `PC_*` site tables in `app/team*.js`).

| Truck and tractor pull | `pull.js` | 6 classes, 5 tyres, pull nights and a points series, runaway sled; setup: gear, driveline, tyre, hitch height, ballast weight and how far back it sits, tyre pressure, throttle stop |
| Mud bog | `bog.js` | 3 classes with sized radiators, 3 tyres, bog nights, AI results |
| Boat drags | `boat.js`: prop or jet pump, hump then plane, wind and chop | 4 classes, 3 hulls, eight-boat ladder nights |
| Road test and track day | `road.js` | 7 chassis, 3 tracks, 4 tyres; a setup sheet shared with Cloudsplitter Peak (roll stiffness and balance, wing, diff, brake bias, tyre pressure, ride height) with three named saved-setup slots, a see-through pale blue ghost of your best lap on that track (and a pale dot on the map) to race against, and after a lap or climb the sheet names the one setup change that gains most (a "Use it" button loads it); track day is the career event (entry fee, needs a certified engine) |

| Dirt oval | `oval.js`: banked-turn limit, friction circle, heat, wear | 3 classes by cubic inches, 2 tracks (clay and paved half-mile), heat then feature |
| 24 Hours of Prairie Sky | `e24.js`: time-stepped endurance with weather, fuel and fixes | 3 classes, 3 modes, a live race with a flag and records; Pit wall: you call the strategy, or a crew chief does (`e24Chief`, `E24_CHIEF` in `sim/e24.js`, since 1.76.102.0) |

Engine swaps (`swap.js`): 20 vehicles in 5 groups and 15 fixes; one fit check shared by the road test, the strip, the salt and the 24 Hours. Pull and bog keep their class trucks.

### Career (`src/app/state.js`, `career.js`, `team.js` and the files named below)

- **Reputation** tiers (`REP_TIERS`): Backyard 0, Local shop 40, Known builder 120, Regional supplier 300, OEM partner 700, Legend 1500, Dynasty 3000.
- **Team seasons** (`team.js`, since 1.76.33.0): the main job. Each season up to five teams call (six with the Showroom hall; a passed offer's place fills when the week closes), each with a driver style (steady hand, hot shoe, rookie, veteran), one series for 8 rounds, a parts budget and a season fee. Since 1.76.59.0 the offers on the table are all different series and lean to different kinds of racing (drag, oval, boat, hill, bog, pull). Since 1.76.60.0 about 85% of offers (`PC_TOP_P`) come from the top two tiers of series the shop has opened, so a big shop mostly sees big-power series. A quarter of the teams are shoestring (`PC_PURSE`: budget ×0.75, fee ×1.12) and a quarter well funded (budget ×1.35, fee ×0.9). About half come with a team rule (`PC_RULES`) drawn from the ones that suit the series. The rules are pump fuel, carburettor, iron block, no power adders, turbo, blower, V8, a displacement cap, a sealed rev limit, 92% reliability, and E85 or methanol. Each pays 5–10% more on the fee and goes on the race-day checklist like a class rule. Series (`PC_EVENTS`) open with rep and only call once the dyno can read the power their field makes: Street drags and Street Stock oval at 0 rep; Gasser drags and Late Model oval at 40; Outlaw radial, Pro stock (naturally aspirated) and Open oval at 120; Pro mod at 300; Funny car at 700; Top dragster at 1,500. The budget pays for the engine: the first build, then any engine change (15% teardown plus any parts that cost more), freshen-up (15%) or rebuild after a breakage (30%); the Tooling hall takes 25% off changes and rebuilds. A new engine needs a certified pull of that exact build. A round runs every other week: race it from the Team tab once it is due, or Close week races it with the season engine as it stands (a broken or missing engine sits it out). Race day is auto-simulated through the real drag and oval physics against the series' fixed rivals, who find 1.5% more power every round; every car scores points (drag 20/14/9/4, oval 20 down to 2). Each round wears the engine 4 reliability points times the style (the Quality lab takes 30% off). Since 1.76.35.0 each round has its own weather: the calendar sets the mean (career week 1 is early May, July is warmest at about 21 °C, and the mean never drops below 5 °C), each round draws its own day around it, and the drags visit a different strip each round (180 to 1,100 m). The team card shows the forecast and race day lands within a couple of degrees of it. The field's power moves with air density to the 0.8 power, so an atmospheric engine feels thin air a little more than the field and a blown one less. Since 1.76.46.0 the season throws in a beat after rounds 2, 4 and 6 for the next round, each at most once a season (`PC_BEATS`): a showcase (double points and round bonus for everyone), an away round (oval only: the other surface), the driver's ask (steady hand: 90% reliability; hot shoe: 5% more power than the season engine; rookie: a fresh engine; veteran: a podium; pays 10% of the season fee and rep if delivered, costs a little rep if not), a tech teardown after a win (the budget pays 12% of the engine and it goes back fresh), a rule change (a rev limit 6% under the season engine's redline for the rest of the season; the field loses 2%), a double-header (the next round the same week, no freshen-up), a rain-out (a week late, 5 °C cooler and damper), an injury (a 0.5-skill stand-in for two rounds), a heat wave (10 °C over the usual), or, after round 6, a double-points finale. Saves from before keep their one round-5 beat. Also since 1.76.46.0, a rescue contract can call (from 40 rep, about one offer in five, one at a time): a team whose supplier went bust after round 3, near the back of the table, for rounds 4 to 8 at a 30% richer fee and 80% of the budget. A rookie gains 0.03 skill every round raced (up to 0.88) and their team pays 15% more. Offers and the season card say where the engine on the bench would run on power against the field. Since 1.76.45.0 the team picks a race-morning map for each round (Safe: 3% less power, +6 reliability, 70% of the wear; Race: as certified; Hero: 4% more power, −8 reliability, 150% of the wear); a steady hand and a rookie drive best on Safe and a hot shoe on Hero (+0.03 skill), and each loses the same on the opposite map; Close week races on the chosen map. A season allows three engines: the first build, every engine change and every rebuild after a breakage use one (a freshen-up doesn't), and the round a fourth or later goes in, the team starts from the back (seeded 8th at the drags, last on the grid at the oval). Each kind of series plugs into `PC_KIND` in `team.js`. Since 1.76.47.0 drivers remember you (`p.drv`): one who finished a season on your engines may call again a season or more later (about one offer in seven), better by 0.04 skill a season (up to 0.92), a tier up where one is open, and a rookie comes back as a steady hand or hot shoe; a driver you took to a title pays 10% more, one you walked out on never calls again, and a veteran retires after three seasons with you. Also since 1.76.47.0 the rival shop that races that kind of event (Hollis Hot Rods the strip and the oval, Marlow & Daughters the hill, Tidewater Diesel & Marine the pull, bog and boats) builds the engine in one rival car in your series: each round says where it finished against yours, and finishing the season ahead of it in the table is a head-to-head win (+2 rep times the tier scale, their rep down 2%), behind it a loss. The mud bog series (`team-bog.js`: Street 4×4 at 0 rep, Modified at 40, Open Super at 120) visits a different pit each round with its own mud, soupy to sticky clay, named in the forecast like the weather; race day is the real bog run against seven rival trucks (each crew picks its tyre and gear in practice), placed by time through the pit or distance stuck, and the extra engine past the allowance runs last in a pit seven trucks have churned (15% deeper, stickier). The boat drags (`team-boat.js`, Outboard at 0 rep, Jet boat at 40, Pro Eliminator at 300, Top Fuel Hydro at 700) are a night of eight boats on a qualifying pass and a heads-up ladder through the real `SIM.boatPass`; each round has its own wind and chop (`SIM.boatWater`), named in the forecast, and the crew picks the prop for the forecast water (the quick `SIM.boatTune`) while race day's water lands a little off it; the extra engine past the allowance is seeded last. The truck and tractor pull (`team-pull.js`): Gas 4×4 and Street diesel at 0 rep, the 2.6 Smoker at 40, the Hot Farm tractor at 120, Super Stock at 300 and the Unlimited Modified at 1,500, so diesel builds have a team to supply; each class's tech card is the race-day rule, race day is the real pull sim (the crew picks the tyre and gear for the dirt, a full pull ties and goes to a pull-off on a heavier sled, points are the pull night's 20/18/16… by place) against seven pullers on dirt that dries and chews up through the night, and the extra engine past the allowance pulls last on the worst dirt. The hill climb series (`team-hill.js`: Open at 0 rep, Time Attack at 40, Cloudsplitter Peak Open at 120, Unlimited at 300) runs rounds 1 to 7 on four made-up hills in turn (Badlands Grade, Kettle Butte, Ghost Ridge, Thunder Pass: 5.4 to 11.8 km, 690 to 3,040 m) and round 8 on Cloudsplitter Peak, one timed run against nine regulars on oval points; its extra engine runs first, on the dustiest road. Since 1.76.80.0 the hill team runs the setup sheet filed for the season engine, and the pull team runs your truck pull sheet's hitch, ballast, tyre pressure, throttle stop and ballast position when that sheet is set to the series' class (the crew still picks the tyre and gear); the team card says whose setup goes racing.
- **Class rules shown early** (`pcHardAll`, `pcRulesHtml`, `pcLimitLine`, `pcLimitGate` in `team.js`, since 1.76.110.0): every offer and the top of a team's card list the series' hard rules (a displacement or cubic-inch limit, a pull class's tech, naturally aspirated, the team's own rule), each ticked or crossed for the engine on the bench and never folded away. While a team is signed, a build that breaks one reads OVER CLASS LIMIT (or NOT CLASS LEGAL) on the engine tag and the Team strip, with the reason on a line under the strip; Build shows the limit under the numbers, Test, the results rail and the results card show it by the Run button, and the first certifying pull of such a build asks before it is paid for.
- **Built around your engine** (`team-fit.js`, since 1.76.69.0): a second section under the teams calling. Once the bench engine holds a clean, certified pull, up to three teams (one per kind of racing) offer a season for that engine as it is, so any sound build has somewhere to race. A series suits it when the engine passes the class rules, it is reliable enough for that kind of racing (80% on the strip, the water and the pull, 85% in the bog, 90% on the oval and the hill), it would run mid-pack or better on power, and its parts cost fits what a team in that series spends. The series can be up to one tier past the ones the shop's rep has opened, and the dyno must read the field's power as for the regular offers. The highest tier that suits wins, then the closest match to the field. The fee is the series' usual; the field's power is set around the engine where the series allows it; the budget covers the build with 30% room for upkeep; the driver's style and any team rule are ones the engine already meets. Nothing is saved: the offers are drawn from the certified build and the season, so certifying the same build again brings the same teams. When nothing suits, the section says what keeps the engine out of the easiest series.
- **Two teams at once** (since 1.76.53.0): from 300 rep (Regional supplier) the Team tab offers a second team while the first is signed (`p.team2`), never in the same series or with the same team or driver. Its round 1 falls on the week the first team isn't racing, so the two race on alternate weeks (a rained-out round that would land on the other team's week slips one more). Each has its own budget, season engine, engine allowance, map and standings; Put the season engine back swaps them on the bench, and an engine change still counts against the team racing it. The header shows both rounds, the strip at the top of the tab follows the team whose engine is on the bench, Close week races every team due, and the car show only runs in a week neither team races. When one season ends the other moves up and offers call for the free place. Achievements: Two at once, and Best in show for the car show.
- **Watch it** (`team-watch.js`, since 1.76.54.0): the drag and oval round result cards have a Watch it button that replays the race in the event's own 3D scene: at the drags the team's last pass of the night (the ladder round it went out in or won, or qualifying) with both lanes rebuilt from reaction time, ET and mph, at the oval the last three laps of the feature (or up to a breakage) with the rivals re-run from the round's seed at their day's power. The result keeps only a few numbers for it (`p.last.watch`, under half a kilobyte). Watching changes nothing in the career: no money, rep, wear, records or event results. Skip jumps to the end, Back to the team returns to the card, and it works on Lite. Since 1.76.100.0 the pull, bog, boat and hill series replay too, from the sim's own trace of the team's run thinned to whole numbers: the hook that settled the pull (the pull-off if there was one), the bog pass, the boat's last pass of the night against the other lane, and the whole hill climb at 4× (1× to 8×).
- **Car show and sound-off** (`team-show.js`, since 1.76.52.0): in an off week (no team round due, or no team) the Team tab has a show the shop can take the certified bench engine to, once a week, for an entry fee of 5% of the purse. Weeks alternate: a show & shine (judges) and a sound-off (the crowd). Each scores four things out of 25: finish (the cam cover, gold best, plus a blower, trumpets, a carb or a billet head you can see; smoke costs points), smoothness (the layout's balance and the damper; diesel clatter costs), idle (the judges want it steady, the crowd wants a big cam's lope) and sound (the judges want a full note near 74 dB and more cylinders, the crowd wants decibels, open cutout counted). A show & shine weights them 1.3/1.1/1.0/0.6, a sound-off 0.6/0.6/1.2/1.6, for a total out of 100. Seven others enter around the show's level (a show & shine 62 at Backyard up to 81 from Legend, a sound-off 40 up to 64, where scores run lower; a stock small-block scores about 82 and 53), and the top three take the purse ($2k at Backyard up to $72k; 100/50/25%) and +2/+1 rep times 1 to 4 by tier. No wear and no team budget.
- **Team pay** (shares of the season fee, `PC_RETAIN`, `PC_RBONUS`, `PC_CBONUS`): 4% for each round raced, a podium bonus of 8/4/2%, a title bonus of 25/12/6% for the top three. Season fees by tier: $60–80k, $130–170k, $280–380k, $600–780k, $1.2–1.6M, $2.4–3.2M. Rep per round and for the title is scaled by the tier (x1, 1.5, 2.5, 4, 6, 9). A top-two finish brings the same team back with 20% more budget and fee.
- **Events** are side income: the purses, records and rep at all ten events are as before, and sponsors and rivals count them.
- **Certification**: a clean dyno pull in the standard cell. It lets an engine race the events and go to a team. The dyno ladder is the starter chassis dyno (400 hp), then the Engine dyno (1,000 hp), Pro dyno cell (2,000 hp, climate control), Outlaw dyno cell (3,500 hp) and Monster dyno cell (15,000 hp / 14,000 rpm), bought as upgrades `dyno2` to `dyno5`.
- **Shop**: 15 upgrades (`UPGRADES`), 3 staff roles (`STAFF`) that become named crew with levels 1 to 5, traits and morale (`crew.js`), 12 R&D projects (`RND`), 3 prestige items (`PRESTIGE`), 32 goals (`GOALS`), a loan.
- **Sponsors** (`career.js`): never ask for an event the shop cannot enter; track day counts as an event.
- **Series banners** (since 1.76.94.0): one picked 3:1 banner per team series in `banners/` (named by the `PC_EVENTS` key, darkened and desaturated so text reads over them; credits in `banners/CREDITS.md`) across the top of team offers and season cards, and one per event on the Events landing cards (`EV_BANNER`). Salt, road and the 24 Hours keep the drawn card art.
- **Sponsor logos** (`spLogoCanvas`, since 1.76.92.0): each brand's picked emblem (`sponsors/`, credits in `sponsors/CREDITS.md`) on a light badge, the name in Chakra Petch; Wild Oat's art is drawn whole. The old drawn marks stand in until the emblem loads.
- **Rival shops** (`rivals.js`): three made-up shops on the reputation board that throw down head-to-head challenges at the strip, the pull, Cloudsplitter Peak, the bog, the boats, the oval, track day, the salt and the 24 Hours. Since 1.76.84.0 a rival can start a feud (half the time it loses a head-to-head to you, a fifth of the time you pass it on the board; one feud at a time, 12 weeks' rest after one ends): it trash-talks in the week report, issues more of the challenges, gloats when it beats you, and a season (16 weeks) after the feud starts it runs a copy of your certified build. It never poaches team offers. Beat it in 2 more head-to-heads to settle the feud for 1.5 times the usual win's rep; it burns out on its own after 40 weeks. The board marks the shop with a Feud chip.
- **Crate engines** (`crate.js`, since 1.76.85.0): side income. List the certified engine (one at a time; listing another replaces it) and each Close week a few buyers order one, with no team or round needed. The shop's level is the top tier of team series its rep has opened. Quality is 45% power against the mark for that tier (`CRATE_HP`: 400, 800, 1,300, 2,100, 3,000, 4,200 hp; 30% of it scores nothing, 110% full marks) and 55% reliability (65% scores nothing, 95% full marks). Orders are a random draw around 1.2 to 3 a week by quality, a little more as rep climbs toward the next tier, and each pays a margin that also grows with quality. A middling build makes about 10% of what a season in the top open series pays, a great one about 20%, a poor one a few percent. The Team tab's Crate engines card shows the price, the margin, the likely orders and what buyers want more of.
- **Legacy** (`legacy.js`): at Legend rep the owner can sell the shop. The sale value becomes Legacy points under their own storage key; points buy permanent perks for the next career.
- **Career dev mode** (Settings, Game; a career save only): a testing switch. While it is on the HUD shows DEV, every upgrade counts as owned (but the Showroom, Tooling and Quality lab team bonuses stay off) and every event class is open whatever the rep. Team series still open with rep. Three buttons add $100,000, add 100 rep, or skip a week.
- **Achievements**: 97 (`ACH`) in 14 groups, bronze, silver and gold. **Trophy room** (`trophy.js`): a dated log of wins per mode.

### Graphics (`src/vis/`, `ENGINE_VIS`)

three.js r128. ### Sound (`src/sound/`)

A Web Audio synth derives a voice from the build (firing order, exhaust, intake, mechanical noise). Forced induction is its own layer: a turbo is a whistle that winds up and coasts down plus, since 1.76.108.0, a rush of air that swells with the wheel; a Roots or screw blower adds an air roar, timing-gear whine and a throb on each firing pulse; a centrifugal adds its step-up gear whine, which rattles at idle. Settings offer **Synth** (your build), **Real** (recordings, the default) or **Blend**, and Lite or Full sound. 76 openly licensed recordings in `sounds/`: engine loops at a measured rpm, pitch-shifted live (every piston layout but the Deltic, plus the rotaries, has its own), and recorded blow-off, backfire and anti-lag one-shots, plus the nitrous hiss (the purge on the line and the shot coming on, since 1.76.105.0), that replace the synth's when loaded; every one is credited in `sounds/CREDITS.md`.

**Drag announcer** (`src/app/announcer.js`, since 1.76.87.0): Hassan, an ElevenLabs-generated voice (recast from Ed in 1.76.125.0 for a quicker call; clips trimmed and sped up by `tools/trim-announcer.sh`), calls the strip. 143 lines in `sounds/announcer/` (script sheet `docs/announcer-script.csv`): 73 race calls in 33 pools (the night opening, each round, staging, a great, perfect or late light, wheelspin in the first 60 ft, red light, win and loss with close and big margins, the other car breaking, your engine or driveline breaking, break-out, centreline, black flag, PB, a clean time trial, qualified, did not qualify, and how the night ended), plus 70 matchup parts stitched into engine-against-engine calls: aspiration, litres, Hemi or two-stroke, fuel, layout, nitrous ("twin turbo two point oh litre inline four, versus Roots blown eight point two litre Hemi methanol V8"). Every rival car and test & tune name has an engine for the call (`ANN_RIVAL`; flavour only, the sim does not read it). A line never repeats back to back. Only the lines picked for the next pass are fetched and decoded (about 20 kB each; a cache keeps the last 28 decoded); the engine ducks while he talks; a red light, wheelspin or the result cuts off whatever he was saying. Settings › Sound › Drag announcer turns him off.

**Ambience and crowd** (`src/app/ambience.js`, since 1.76.90.0): ten 18-second ElevenLabs ambience loops in `sounds/amb/`, one per venue plus the shop, and eight crowd one-shots in `sounds/crowd/`. The Events tab plays the bed for the event that is set up, every other tab plays the shop; beds crossfade when you move and stop when the page is hidden. Each event's finish calls `crowdAfter` (a win cheers, a loss claps or gasps if it was close, a breakage groans, a full pull, a cleared bog pit and a summit have their own), and a certified career dyno pull gets a shop clap. Its own level in Settings › Sound › Ambience & crowd (`S.ambVol`, 40 by default); while a bed plays the synth does not idle-suspend the audio (`ENGINE_SOUND.holdAwake`).

**Foley** (`src/app/foley.js`, since 1.76.91.0): ten short ElevenLabs one-shots in `sounds/fx/`. A Build change gets a workshop tool: the torque wrench for fasteners (rod bolts, head studs, gasket, bearings), the impact gun for big swaps (layout, block, heads, crank, induction, fuel, exhaust), the ratchet for machining and other parts, a socket click for a slider; one tool sound per 250 ms at most, and sliders only when let go. The dyno rollers spin up when a dyno test starts and spin down when it ends; the drag and salt parachute pops; a hill climb car throws gravel when it runs wide, slides onto the bank or goes over the edge; a drag boat leaves with a rooster tail; the mud bog layers a mud splat on the synth's slaps at the pit entry, ridges, ruts and landings. Clips use the ambience cache (`ambLoad`), are fetched ahead for the tab or event you are on, follow the main Sound volume (`S.vol`), play two at a time at most, and vary a little in pitch and level.

### Save system

- Everything lives in the browser's localStorage under five keys: `pew2-save` (the current game, settings included), `pew2-slot1` to `pew2-slot3` (three save slots, `SLOTS`), `pew2-legacy` (Legacy points). All access is wrapped in try/catch.
- **Back up** turns all five into one text code: `PEW1:` + LZW-packed JSON in base64url + a 6-character checksum. **Restore** checks the whole code first and writes nothing if it is damaged, cut off, from another game (`GG1:`, `RS2:`) or from a newer version (`PEW3:` and up). Older one-game `PEW2:` codes still load. A restore that fails half-way puts every key back.
- **Save backup file** writes the same `PEW1:` code into `prairie-engine-works-backup-YYYY-MM-DD.txt` (the share sheet where the browser offers it, so iPhone gets "Save to Files"; otherwise a download). **Load backup file** reads one and runs it through Restore, with the same checks and confirm.
- The game has its own web address (pew.longlight.games), so its own storage pot, which the browser may clear when a phone runs low. After the first save of a session with a game going (or a backup or restore) the game asks the browser to keep its storage (`navigator.storage.persist`, once per page load). Settings › Save shows one line: storage kept or not, and the last backup date.
- `S.bkAt` (in `pew2-save`, no new key) is when the last backup was taken (code copied or file saved); a restore keeps the newer of the two. With a career past week 3 or two or more blueprints and no backup in 14 days, a quiet bar offers **Saves** once per session, at a calm moment (after Close week, or once the session settles), never over a run, an event, a sheet or Settings.
- `check-savecode.js` runs these paths in node with a pretend localStorage (15 checks).

### Offline and install

`sw.js` (generated by `build.js`) precaches the page, `scenes.js` (the event scenes, which the page fetches in the background after the garage is up, since 1.76.99.0), manifest and icons under a cache named `pew-shell-v<VERSION>-<hash>`, keeps three.js and fonts in a long-lived library cache, and caches sounds as they are fetched. A new version installs in the background and the page shows "New version ready". Settings > App offers Install (or the iOS Add to Home Screen tip).
